Retained only the single irreducible \textbf{Load-Use Hazard} (where an instruction in ID reads the destination of a memory load currently in EX), inserting exactly 1 stall cycle before forwarding the loaded data from MEM/WB into EX.

The forwarded core completed the RV32I conformance suite in 103,268 cycles (a 38.8\% cycle reduction over the unforwarded pipeline) and the Zmmul suite in 14,714 cycles (a 55.3\% reduction).

        The standalone core critical path is $1.589\,\text{ns}$ ($629.2\,\text{MHz}$) vs. $1.362\,\text{ns}$ ($734.0\,\text{MHz}$) due to bypass multiplexer insertion on ALU operands.
        \item \textit{Benchmark Rerun and Empirical Speedup (Table \ref{tab:riscv_all_benchmarks})}: Re-executed CoreMark 1.0, Dhrystone 2.1, and DSP workloads on the forwarded core:

        Demonstrated that spending +2.7\% silicon area eliminates $>60\%$ of all pipeline stalls and drives application throughput up by over 40\%.

    \item \textbf{Physical Synthesis Realities: EDA Baseline Multipliers vs. Explicit Architectures in Chapter 15}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{EDA Baseline Multiplier Mechanics (Section \ref{sec:multiplier_eda_synthesis_realities})}: Documented the internal EDA transformation of HDL multiplication expressions (\texttt{*} / \texttt{\$mul}) in Yosys and Berkeley ABC. Proved that Yosys does not synthesize naive shift-and-add logic; the \texttt{alumacc} pass transforms operations into multiply-accumulate macro-cells (\texttt{\$macc}), and \texttt{maccmap} decomposes \texttt{\$macc} into a bit-slice carry-save compressor tree with a Brent-Kung parallel prefix vector-merging adder (\texttt{\$lcu\_brent\_kung}), followed by ABC AIG cut-based delay rewriting. Revealed that supporting RISC-V \texttt{Zmmul} dynamic signedness caused Yosys to instantiate two complete 64-bit carry-save compressor trees behind an output multiplexer, explaining the 10,240 cell count and $1,083.36\,\mu\text{m}^2$ baseline area.
        \item \textit{Why Array Multipliers Synthesized to Lowest Delay ($1.968\,\text{ns}$)}: Demonstrated that single-gate 2-input AND partial product generation requires merely $\approx 15\,\text{ps}$ in ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET without recoding or multiplexing overhead, allowing ABC's AIG cut-based optimization to balance the flat multi-operand sum into a shallow reduction tree that reaches the vector-merging adder earlier than architectures with complex front-ends.
        \item \textit{The Radix-4 Booth Trade-Off}: Detailed the front-end recoding penalty (3-bit window decode, 5:1 multiplexing, negation logic = $\approx 150\text{--}200\,\text{ps}$) versus tree reduction savings (saving 2 carry-save levels = $\approx 80\,\text{ps}$). At 32 bits, the front-end penalty exceeds tree savings, making unpipelined Booth slightly slower ($2.160\,\text{ns}$ vs. $1.968\,\text{ns}$). However, Booth achieves a massive \textbf{57.1\% area reduction} ($464.45\,\mu\text{m}^2$, lowest of all architectures) and eliminates routing congestion. Demonstrated that for 64-bit and 128-bit datapaths, logarithmic tree savings easily dominate the constant front-end penalty.
        \item \textit{Wallace Tree Limitations in Standard Cells}: Analyzed why explicit structural netlists of full adders restrict ABC Boolean optimization and how irregular, non-planar wiring creates routing congestion and parasitic wire RC delays in deep-submicron FinFET nodes ($2.089\,\text{ns}$).
        \item \textit{Redundant Binary Multipliers in Standard Cells vs. Custom DSP Silicon}: Explained why automated standard-cell synthesis penalizes RBM ($1,072.65\,\mu\text{m}^2$, $2.246\,\text{ns}$) due to dual-rail encoding and scattered standard-cell placement, contrasting this with custom DSP datapaths (e.g., the multithreaded Sandblaster DSP) where pitch-matched bit-slice tiling and modular H-tree interconnects provide deterministic wire lengths and noise immunity. Highlighted that DSP multiply-accumulate (MAC) loops accumulate directly in redundant binary format across cycles in constant $O(1)$ time, eliminating the final two's complement subtractor from the inner loop critical path.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Performance Multipliers in Chapter 15 and ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET Synthesis for Zmmul Acceleration in Chapter 19}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{High-Performance Multiplier Architectures (Chapter 15, Section \ref{sec:high_performance_multipliers})}: Added a comprehensive section covering the fundamentals of 3-phase hardware multiplication (partial product generation, reduction, and final carry-propagate addition) across five distinct architectures:
        (1) \textbf{Array Multiplier}: 2D planar systolic array of carry-save cells with $O(N)$ propagation delay and $O(N^2)$ area.
        (2) \textbf{Radix-4 Modified Booth Multiplier}: Overlapping 3-bit window recoding with digit set $\{-2, -1, 0, +1, +2\}$, cutting the number of partial product rows in half from $N$ to $\lceil (N+1)/2 \rceil$ (32 bits to 17 rows) and slashing reduction hardware.
        (3) \textbf{Wallace and Dadda Carry-Save Trees}: Fast 3:2 and 2:2 compression trees reducing partial products in $O(\log N)$ logic depth, contrasting eager Wallace reduction with deferred Dadda compression, and analyzing deep-submicron interconnect routing congestion trade-offs.
        (4) \textbf{Redundant Binary Multiplier (RBM)}: Formulated the Redundant Binary Signed-Digit (RBSD) representation with digit set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ encoded as $(x^+, x^-)$, constant-time $O(1)$ carry-free Redundant Binary Adder (RBA) cells using adjacent lower digit condition testing ($p_{i-1} = (x_{i-1} + y_{i-1} > 0)$), and a balanced binary reduction tree ($2 \to 1$ at every node) of depth $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil = 5$. Highlighted its historical and practical prominence in high-performance digital signal processor (DSP) design (such as the Sandblaster DSP architecture), where strict layout modularity, predictable interconnect lengths, and uniform bit-slice pitch matching avoid the wiring chaos of Wallace trees.
        \item \textit{Chisel Implementation and Verification}: Implemented all multiplier topologies in Chisel 7.15.0 (\texttt{scabook.multipliers}), verified 100\% functional correctness against comprehensive signed and unsigned arithmetic vectors (\texttt{MultiplierSuiteSpec.scala}), and generated clean SystemVerilog netlists (\texttt{generators.GenerateMultipliers}).
        \item \textit{ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET Physical ASIC Synthesis of Multipliers (Chapter 19, Section \ref{riscvMultiplierSynthesis})}: Synthesized all five 32-bit multiplier architectures to gate-level netlists using Yosys and ABC targeting the predictive ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET standard cell library (\texttt{asap7sc7p5t\_28}, RVT, $0.70\,\text{V}$, $25^\circ\text{C}$, Typical TT corner, $C_{\text{load}} = 2.0\,\text{fF}$):

        \item \textit{Multiplier Selection, Chisel Integration, and Pipeline Balancing (Section 19.5)}: Selected the Radix-4 Booth Multiplier as the primary area-optimized multiplier for standard-cell cores (while noting RBM for regular DSP datapaths).     \item \textbf{4-Stage Pipelined RV32I Baseline Implementation, Architectural Conformance, ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET Synthesis, and Benchmark Evaluation (Chapter 19)}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{4-Stage Pipelined Architecture (\texttt{RiscvPipelined.scala}, Section 19.2)}: Designed and implemented a 4-stage pipelined processor core (\texttt{RiscvPipelined}) for the base \texttt{RV32I} architecture in Chisel 7.15.0, partitioning execution into Instruction Fetch (IF), Instruction Decode/Operand Fetch (ID), Execute/Branch Resolution (EX), and Memory/Write-Back (MEM/WB) stages operating directly against unbuffered memory buses without caches. Added configurable hardware multiplier parameterization (\texttt{enableZmmul: Boolean = false}).
        \item \textit{Baseline Hazard Detection Unit (No Forwarding)}: Implemented an active Hazard Detection Unit enforcing a pure non-forwarding baseline where all RAW hazards stall until writeback commits in the MEM/WB stage. In the absence of forwarding, distance-1 RAW dependencies incur 2 stall cycles and distance-2 RAW dependencies incur 1 stall cycle by freezing \texttt{pcReg} and \texttt{if\_id} while injecting bubbles into \texttt{id\_ex}. Evaluated branch/jump target resolution in the EX stage with pipeline flushing (2-cycle taken branch penalty, 0-cycle not-taken penalty).
        \item \textit{Architectural Conformance Verification (100\% Pass Rate, Section 19.3)}: Verified 100\% pass rate across all 38 official RISC-V \texttt{RV32I} architectural test suites (168,638 simulation cycles) and all 4 official \texttt{Zmmul} test suites (32,892 cycles) with exact word-for-word golden signature matching against Spike golden references (\texttt{RiscvPipelinedConformanceSpec.scala}).
        \item \textit{ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET Predictive PDK Physical ASIC Synthesis (Sections 19.3 \& 19.4)}: Synthesized all processor cores and ALUs to the predictive ASAP7 7\,nm FinFET standard cell library (\texttt{asap7sc7p5t\_28}, 7.5-track standard cells, RVT, $V_{dd} = 0.70\,\text{V}$, $25^\circ\text{C}$, Typical TT corner, $C_{\text{load}} = 2.0\,\text{fF}$) using Yosys and ABC.
        The single-cycle \texttt{RV32I} core occupies $904.24\,\mu\text{m}^2$ (9,730 cells, 1,023 DFFs).
        The 4-stage pipelined \texttt{RV32I} core occupies $1,128.16\,\mu\text{m}^2$ (11,584 cells, 1,434 DFFs, +24.8\% area).
        \item \textit{The 4-Step Pipeline Delay Model (SRAM Fetch, Decode, Execute, SRAM Write-Back)}: Decomposed execution into four physical steps: Step 1 SRAM Fetch ($T_{\text{fetch}} = 0.550\,\text{ns}$), Step 2 Decode and Register Read ($T_{\text{decode}} = 0.540\,\text{ns}$), Step 3 Execute ($T_{\text{execute}} = 1.363\,\text{ns}$ for pure RV32I, $2.462\,\text{ns}$ for Zmmul), and Step 4 SRAM Data Memory and Write-Back ($T_{\text{sram\_wb}} = 0.600\,\text{ns}$).
        In the single-cycle Fetch-Execute (FE) core, the clock period is the sum of all four steps: $T_{\text{clk, FE}} = 0.550 + 0.540 + 1.363 + 0.600 = \mathbf{3.053\,\text{ns}}$ ($327.5\,\text{MHz}$).
        In the 4-stage pipelined core, the clock period is the maximum step delay: $T_{\text{clk, pipe}} = \max(0.550, 0.540, 1.363, 0.600) = \mathbf{1.363\,\text{ns}}$ ($733.7\,\text{MHz}$).
        The clock period delta is $\Delta T = T_{\text{clk, FE}} - T_{\text{clk, pipe}} = \mathbf{1.690\,\text{ns}}$ ($-55.4\%$), delivering a \textbf{2.240$\times$ frequency multiplier} (+124.0\%).
        \item \textit{Microarchitectural Multiplier Case Study (Zmmul Extension, Section 19.4)}: Standalone ALU synthesis revealed that the $32 \times 32$ multiplier logic array caused an \textbf{11.5$\times$ ALU area explosion} ($103.31\,\mu\text{m}^2 \to 1,186.67\,\mu\text{m}^2$) and an intrinsic delay of $2.148\,\text{ns}$ (+105.3\% delay). Placing the multiplier monolithically in the EX stage expanded the EX stage delay to $2.462\,\text{ns}$, causing a \textbf{frequency collapse} (pipelined core frequency dropped from $733.7\,\text{MHz}$ to $406.2\,\text{MHz}$, shrinking pipelining frequency gain to a modest +68.7\%, $1.69\times$, compared to $2.24\times$ in pure RV32I). While hardware multiplication achieved an 8.1$\times$ dynamic instruction reduction on the DSP benchmark (804 vs. 6,511 instructions), the study demonstrated Amdahl's Law for clock periods and motivated multi-cycle or pipelined multiplier units.
        \item \textit{Comprehensive Empirical Workload Evaluation (Section 19.4)}: Executed DSP kernels, Dhrystone 2.1 (20 iterations), and EEMBC CoreMark 1.0 (1 iteration, 2\,KB data footprint, 874,891 instructions) on both cores with 100\% signature and CRC validation:
        (1) \textbf{DSP Kernel (Soft Mul)}: 6,511 insts, Single = 6,511 cyc (CPI 1.00), Pipe = 12,575 cyc (CPI 1.93, +93.1\% stalls). $T_{\text{exec, FE}} = 19,878\,\text{ns}$, $T_{\text{exec, pipe}} = 17,140\,\text{ns}$. Speedup = \textbf{1.16$\times$}.
        (2) \textbf{DSP Kernel (Hard Mul)}: 804 insts, Single = 804 cyc (CPI 1.00), Pipe = 1,308 cyc (CPI 1.63, +62.7\% stalls). $T_{\text{exec, FE}} = 3,338\,\text{ns}$, $T_{\text{exec, pipe}} = 3,220\,\text{ns}$. Speedup = \textbf{1.04$\times$}.
        (3) \textbf{Dhrystone 2.1}: 13,826 insts, Single = 13,826 cyc (CPI 1.00), Pipe = 24,311 cyc (CPI 1.76, +75.8\% stalls). $T_{\text{exec, FE}} = 42,211\,\text{ns}$, $T_{\text{exec, pipe}} = 33,136\,\text{ns}$. Single: 0.824 DMIPS/MHz (269.9 DMIPS). Pipe: 0.468 DMIPS/MHz (343.4 DMIPS). Speedup = \textbf{1.27$\times$}.
        (4) \textbf{CoreMark 1.0}: 874,891 insts, Single = 874,891 cyc (CPI 1.00), Pipe = 1,643,060 cyc (CPI 1.88, +87.8\% stalls). $T_{\text{exec, FE}} = 2,671,042\,\text{ns}$, $T_{\text{exec, pipe}} = 2,239,491\,\text{ns}$. Single: 1.143 CoreMark/MHz (374.3 CoreMarks). Pipe: 0.608 CoreMark/MHz (446.1 CoreMarks). Speedup = \textbf{1.19$\times$}.
        \item \textit{Why Ideal Pipeline Speedup is Inaccessible in Practice}: Analyzed why the pipelined \texttt{RV32I} runs only a tiny bit faster ($1.04\times\text{--}1.27\times$) without forwarding:
        (1) \textbf{Stage Delay Imbalance}: Logic is indivisible; the 1.363\,ns EX stage bounds frequency, limiting frequency scaling to $3.053 / 1.363 = 2.240\times$ and eliminating 44.0\% of theoretical speedup before any code runs.
        (2) \textbf{Sequential Register Overhead}: Flip-flop setup, clock-to-Q, and clock skew impose $\approx 55\,\text{ps}$ of dead delay per stage ($>200\,\text{ps}$ across 4 stages in 7\,nm FinFET).
        (3) \textbf{Hazard-Induced CPI Expansion}: Without forwarding, RAW stalls and branch flush bubbles degrade CPI to $1.63\text{--}1.93$ (+63\% to +93\% cycle penalty), which consumes almost the entire $2.24\times$ frequency multiplier.
        \item \textit{Architectural Benchmark Taxonomy}: Formulated the architectural prerequisites of SPEC CPU (MMU with virtual memory paging, POSIX OS / Linux, multi-GB RAM, glibc), proving why SPEC CPU cannot run on bare-metal microcontroller cores and contrasting it with freestanding embedded suites (Dhrystone, CoreMark, Embench-IoT).
        \item \textit{Analytical Exercises}: Formulated Problems 19.3, 19.5, and 19.6 with complete step-by-step mathematical solutions calculating no-forwarding stall penalties, ASAP7 silicon area and frequency trade-offs, CoreMark speedup decomposition under the 4-step pipeline delay model, and benchmark architectural requirements.
